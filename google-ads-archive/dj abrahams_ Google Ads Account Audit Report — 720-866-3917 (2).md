## 1\. COMPROMISED SITE (cervical-center.com)

## What it means: Google has detected that cervical-center.com's code has been manipulated by a third party — essentially, Google believes the site has been hacked or injected with malicious code. According to Google's policy, this includes things like scripts that transmit user data without consent, credit card skimmers, malware installation, unauthorized redirects, or running on a CMS with known exploited security vulnerabilities. Why this is the most urgent issue: This isn't just an ad policy problem — it means the website itself may be actively compromised and could be harming visitors. Google's Safe Browsing may also be flagging the site in organic search results. How to fix it: First, run cervical-center.com through Google's Safe Browsing Site Status tool at transparencyreport.google.com/safe-browsing/search to see if Google has flagged the domain. Then check Google Search Console for the domain — run the Security Issues Report, which will identify specific malicious software or security problems. Have your web developer or a security specialist do a full audit looking for injected scripts, unauthorized redirects, outdated CMS/plugins, or any code referencing unknown external domains. After cleaning the site, update all CMS software, themes, and plugins to the latest versions. Then submit a review through Google Search Console to get the site removed from the Safe Browsing threat list. Once that's cleared, appeal the ad disapprovals in Google Ads using the "Made changes to comply with policy" option. Allow up to 72 hours for Google to re-crawl and re-evaluate the landing page. Best practices going forward: Keep the CMS and all plugins updated at all times. Set up email notifications in Google Search Console to catch security issues early. Consider adding a Web Application Firewall (WAF) through services like Cloudflare or Sucuri.

## 2\. MISREPRESENTATION (cervical-center.com)

## What it means: Google has determined that your ads or landing pages are misleading users about your identity, affiliations, qualifications, or what you're offering. For your account specifically, the disapproval reason noted "Misrepresentation \+2 more," which suggests multiple sub-violations under this umbrella policy. Common triggers for healthcare advertisers: The most likely triggers in your case are misleading representation and possibly unclear relevance. Misleading representation means making statements that obscure or omit material information about who you are, your affiliations, or your qualifications — or providing a business name in the ad that doesn't clearly represent the actual business. Unclear relevance means the ad content doesn't match what users find on the landing page. For your account, this could be happening because the ad copy references "The Cervical Spine Center" but the landing page on cervical-center.com may not clearly match, or may not prominently display the actual business entity, its medical credentials, physical address, or provider qualifications. It could also be triggered if the site makes claims about treatments, expertise, or outcomes without proper substantiation. How to fix it: Make sure the landing page clearly identifies the actual medical practice/business entity behind "The Cervical Spine Center." Display physician credentials, board certifications, and practice affiliations prominently. The business name in ads must exactly match what's on the landing page and be the actual legal/operating name. Ensure the landing page has complete contact information including a physical address, phone number, and clear "About Us" content. Remove any claims about being "the best" or "\#1" or any superlative that can't be independently verified. Make sure the URL domain matches the business being advertised — if cervical-center.com is a sub-brand of NY Brain & Spine Surgery, that relationship should be clearly stated on the site. After making changes, appeal using the "Made changes to comply with policy" option. Important note: Google states that repeated Misrepresentation violations can escalate to account suspension with 7 days' notice. Don't let this linger.

## 3\. UNRELIABLE CLAIMS (cervical-center.com sitelinks \+ ads)

## What it means: Your ads or landing pages make claims that are inaccurate, unproven, or entice users with an improbable result presented as the likely outcome. For healthcare advertisers, this policy is especially strict. Specific healthcare triggers from Google's policy: Google specifically prohibits making unproven claims of cures for incurable medical conditions (examples they give include arthritis, diabetes, Alzheimer's, and cancer). They also prohibit "miracle cure" language, "cure-all" claims, and content that promotes harmful health claims or contradicts scientific consensus. If you guarantee certain results, you must have a clear and accessible refund policy. If you use patient testimonials claiming specific results, you must include a visible disclaimer stating results are not guaranteed and can vary. What's likely happening in your account: Your ad copy and/or the cervical-center.com landing pages likely contain language around treatment outcomes, success rates, or patient results that Google considers "unreliable." In spine surgery advertising, common triggers include phrases like "guaranteed pain relief," "100% success rate," implied cure language for chronic conditions, before/after testimonials without disclaimers, or claims like "best results" or "most advanced treatment." How to fix it: Audit all ad headlines, descriptions, and sitelink text for outcome-based claims. Replace absolute language ("cure," "fix," "eliminate") with qualified language ("may help," "treatment options for," "consult with a specialist about"). Any patient testimonials on landing pages need a visible disclaimer stating results vary and are not guaranteed. Remove or add proper context to any success rate statistics — if you cite a study, link to it. Avoid superlatives like "best," "\#1," or "world-renowned" unless you can back them with third-party verification. For sitelinks specifically — all four of your Cervical Spine Center sitelinks were disapproved, so review the sitelink text and the pages they link to. After making changes, submit appeals on each disapproved asset individually.
